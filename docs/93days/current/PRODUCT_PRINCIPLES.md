# 93 Days — PRODUCT PRINCIPLES

Status: **CURRENT CONTEXT MAP**
Updated: **2026-10-03**

This file keeps important product ideas visible without pretending every concept seed is already canon.
It separates **FIXED**, **WORKING**, and **SEED** decisions.

## 1. FIXED / strong current direction

### The player lives a summer, not a checklist

The 93 days are a narrative frame, not an efficiency timer.
Missing content is allowed and often desirable.

A missed event may become:

- a rumor;
- a later consequence;
- another person's story;
- a changed relationship;
- an opportunity that no longer exists.

The player should feel “this was my summer”, not “I cleared 82% of quests”.

### The city is a character

At first the city should feel large, illogical and foreign.
Over time it becomes familiar without ever becoming completely solved.

It is alive: transport, industry, students, shops, port, nightlife, ordinary work and daily routines continue.
Mysteries exist inside ordinary life rather than replacing it.

### Old city vs new city is a conflict of perspectives

Modernization is a long-running frame rather than one mandatory central quest.

Local disputes can involve:

- tower reconstruction;
- market;
- cinema;
- waterfront;
- unfinished metro;
- workshops/factories;
- courtyards;
- transport.

Each side needs plausible interests.
Do not encode one faction as automatically morally correct.

### Investigation is optional

The player can spend the summer around:

- relationships;
- music;
- work;
- parties;
- activism;
- exploration;
- money;
- early internet/community;
- city mysteries;
- simply learning how to live in the city.

## 2. Social and relationship principles

### Interpersonal state != generalized social beliefs

Interpersonal state may move relatively quickly:

- familiarity;
- trust;
- affection;
- attraction;
- obligation;
- grievance;
- fear;
- respect.

Generalized social beliefs should move more slowly and require repeated or high-salience evidence.

### Provenance matters

Rumors and reports retain:

- who said it;
- who witnessed it;
- source trust/credibility;
- whether the player later provides firsthand evidence.

One direct encounter should not erase the existence of an earlier rumor.

### Biography changes interpretation

The same event may be interpreted differently depending on biography, social network, prior history and memory.

Positive contact is not guaranteed to improve a group attitude.
Negative contact is not guaranteed to worsen it.

### Memory is history, not a disposable flag

Memory may:

- decay in salience;
- be reinforced;
- remain contradictory;
- support reconciliation or renewed grievance.

The city near Day 90 should contain accumulated shared history.

### Research social graph != game relationship graph

Research edge colors describe how typical an intersection between social environments is.
They do not mean friendship, hostility or deterministic behavior.

A socially “unlikely” bridge can be an especially valuable NPC.

## 3. Everyday-life principles

### Time follows meaningful actions

Do not run a constant anxiety timer.
Time advances primarily through meaningful actions, travel, conversations, waiting, work, sleep and other real costs.

Scheduled Presence remains distinct from Actual Presence.

### Body is a decision system

Fatigue, sleep debt, satiety, heavy meals, injury and recovery should matter when they create choices,
not as constant micromanagement.

### Carrying capacity changes planning

Pockets, small bag, portfolio/backpack and larger luggage can change what the player can carry.

**Working design direction:** morning preparation can become a small planning ritual:
what to take, what to leave, where the player expects to go.

### Objects can acquire personal history

Items may become meaningful because of where they came from, who gave them, or what happened with them.
Do not reduce every object to pure inventory statistics.

## 4. Phone / early internet

### FIXED direction: phone as social infrastructure

The button phone is not only a menu.

It can support:

- calls;
- SMS;
- changing plans;
- meeting coordination;
- saved contacts;
- later social consequences.

**Working product idea:** the phonebook gradually becomes a practical “map of relationships”.

### Early internet as a second social layer

The setting allows early forums/computer access.

A character may present differently online and in person.
This can create social bridges, misunderstandings and information paths that do not require physical co-location.

Do not build a separate internet simulation engine before an authored arc needs it.

## 5. Player identity

### Past may be partially authored through play

The protagonist's history does not need to be completely fixed at character creation.
Dialogue answers and choices may establish parts of it.

### SEED: internal “facets”

Earlier concept work proposed internal voices/aspects such as:

- Observer;
- Conversationalist;
- Pressure/Drive;
- Dreamer;
- Skeptic;
- Wanderer;
- Self-control.

This is **SEED / NOT A CURRENT SYSTEM**.
Do not implement it merely because it exists in concept history.
Revisit only if multi-week content proves a need for an internal commentary layer.

## 6. Home hub

**Working decision:** student dormitory in the central/student transition zone.

The dorm is a social location, not just a save/sleep room.

Potential micro-locations include:

- room;
- corridor;
- kitchen;
- common/TV room;
- study room;
- laundry/drying room;
- duty desk;
- entrance benches/smoking area;
- cheap nearby food;
- small sports area;
- era-appropriate shared phone/payphone.

Exact dorm type/roommates remain content decisions.

## 7. Ending direction

The final state should emerge from the lived summer.

Candidate dimensions include:

- relationships;
- social circles/bridges;
- trust/grievance;
- knowledge and contested stories;
- money/work;
- habitual places;
- attended/missed events;
- city-change involvement;
- people who arrived/left;
- whether/how the player leaves the city.

Exact endings remain deliberately unfrozen until multi-week production/playtests show which dimensions
actually produce meaningful player histories.

## 8. Rule for future concepts

When a new idea appears, mark it explicitly as one of:

- **FIXED** — current product invariant/accepted direction;
- **WORKING** — preferred current content decision, still revisable;
- **SEED** — interesting idea, not implementation justification.

Do not allow SEED material from archives to silently become requirements.
